HOW WE WANT TO WORK

High expectations.
Human beings.

We value people who can enter an unfamiliar domain, learn quickly, and make something work. Bring breadth, and something you’ve gone deep on.

01

Start with the problem.

A customer’s first request is the beginning of the conversation. Understand the outcome, the people involved, and what might fail. Question the brief when it needs questioning. Work out what is true and what to test next.

02

See the whole system.

A useful feature can make the wider workflow worse. Look at the hand-offs, incentives, and exceptions around it. When a problem keeps returning, turn what you learned into something the team can use again.

03

Have a point of view. Keep learning.

Make a call with the information you have. Explain your thinking and let new evidence change it. Study work you admire, invite critique, and keep practising. Judgement and taste take work.

04

Move while the idea is still fresh.

Make reversible decisions quickly. Put the smallest useful version in front of a customer and learn from it. Take more care when a decision puts trust, privacy, or significant money at risk. Speed comes from short loops and fewer idle days.

05

Care enough to finish.

Try the awkward edge case. Read the error message. Sit with the person using what you built. If the system is confusing or makes no difference to their work, we still have work to do.

06

Be ambitious. Be good company.

We want to do exceptional work and compete on its quality. Give credit freely, help without keeping score, and disagree without diminishing the person. Skill without decency is too expensive.

07

Own the outcome. Ask for help.

Early work comes with unanswered questions. Seek context, make sensible assumptions, and bring people in before you get stuck. When something goes wrong, say it early, repair it, and change how we work so we learn from it.

08

Keep the parts that make you, you.

Bring your strong opinions, odd interests, and questions. Care about the mission and something beyond it too. Work should have ambition without consuming your entire identity.
